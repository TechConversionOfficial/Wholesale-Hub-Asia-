## About the Business and Project

The public Tech Conversion portfolio identifies Wholesale Hub Asia as a jewellery-wholesale ecommerce website. Its stated assortment brings together bangles, imitation jewellery, bridal collections and fashion accessories. This is different from a software product named Wholesale Hub, a generic wholesale marketplace or another fashion retailer with a similar name.

Jewellery shopping depends heavily on design, finish, colour and occasion. A retailer sourcing bridal products may evaluate collection breadth and quantity requirements, whereas an individual shopper may focus on colour, style and matching pieces for a ceremony. A well-structured website can make those decisions easier by giving visitors sensible product families and reliable product information.

## The Challenge

A store selling imitation jewellery and seasonal accessories can have many visually similar products, with important differences in colour, size, material, set contents, finish and intended occasion. A customer should not have to inspect dozens of unrelated product cards to locate bridal bangles or everyday accessories.

For wholesale buying, catalogue clarity matters as much as visual appeal. Retail buyers often need to assess assortment, order quantities, current prices, packaging and product consistency. Wedding and occasion buyers may look for coordinated sets, colour combinations and styling detail. Those needs suggest an ecommerce experience with clear collections, useful images and dependable purchasing or quotation information rather than a generic landing page.

This describes the design problem implied by the agency's portfolio positioning. It is not a documented audit of an earlier production website.

## How the Ecommerce Experience Is Positioned

The following describes the project's published scope and suitable ecommerce design priorities. It does not claim that each individual feature was deployed or tested on a live production domain.

1. **Organise the product range around meaningful collections.** Provide recognisable routes for bangles, imitation jewellery, bridal styles and general accessories rather than placing everything in one catalogue.
2. **Give the products space to sell visually.** Use authentic product photography with consistent framing and enough detail to convey design, stonework, size and finish.
3. **Make bridal discovery intentional.** Where supported by actual inventory, help visitors explore wedding-ready pieces and coordinated sets rather than forcing them through everyday fashion accessories first.
4. **Account for wholesale purchasing needs.** Where relevant to the business model, show minimum order quantities, pack sizes, volume pricing or enquiry information clearly, without assuming a particular discount or trade-account system exists.
5. **Keep product information practical.** Accurate descriptions should cover material, finish, size, colour, included pieces, care, stock and variations that affect the order.
6. **Support mobile catalogue browsing.** Jewellery buyers often discover products through social channels or shared links. Category navigation, photographs and purchase actions should remain usable on narrow screens.
7. **Communicate ordering policies transparently.** Confirm delivery locations, shipping charges, dispatch windows, return eligibility and product-condition policies with the client.
8. **Provide an accessible next step.** Shopping, a quotation request or a sales enquiry may all be relevant, but the deployed pathway needs to be verified before claiming it works end to end.
9. **Keep branding recognisable without overpowering merchandise.** Typographic hierarchy, appropriate colour use and clean product-card spacing should keep the jewellery itself as the focal point.
10. **Make commercial claims verifiable.** Testimonials, supplier authorisations, product counts, shipping promises and sales improvements should only be published with client-approved evidence.

## Quality, Search and Commercial Standards to Review

These are appropriate audit criteria, not statements that independent certification or testing has occurred.

- **[Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals):** evaluate collection images, product cards, mobile layout stability and interactions.
- **[WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):** review keyboard-accessible navigation, filtering controls, readable labels, alt text and focus visibility.
- **[Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials):** review crawlable product and collection pages, unique descriptions, logical internal links and canonicalisation.
- **[Schema.org](https://schema.org/):** assess accurate Product, Offer, BreadcrumbList and Organisation structured data where supported by verified catalogue information.
- **Jewellery accuracy:** confirm material and plating descriptions, dimensions, quantities, photographs and expected colour differences; distinguish imitation jewellery from precious metals.
- **Wholesale information:** clearly explain whether there are trade accounts, minimum orders, quantity breaks, tax invoices and business-registration requirements, if applicable.
- **Customer policy and privacy:** check delivery coverage, refunds, payments, order confirmation and secure handling of personal data.
- **Photography rights:** only publish product photographs, brand logos or customer images with the required rights and consent.
